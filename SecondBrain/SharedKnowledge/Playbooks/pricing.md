## Pattern Library

### Pattern: Anchor high before revealing actual price
**Evidence:** Presenting a higher "without us" cost before your price makes your price feel smaller.
**Application:**
- "This typically costs companies $X/year in [time/resources/errors]"
- Then reveal your price
**Confidence:** High

---

### Pattern: Three tiers create a middle choice
**Evidence:** Two options force an either/or. Three options make the middle feel like the "smart" choice.
**Application:**
- Tier 1: Stripped down (makes middle look better)
- Tier 2: **Your target** (most features, right price)
- Tier 3: Premium (enterprise/custom, makes middle look accessible)
**Confidence:** High

---

### Pattern: Monthly vs annual framing matters
**Evidence:** Annual pricing shown as "monthly equivalent" ($X/month, billed annually) performs better than showing the lump annual sum.
**Application:** Always show price as monthly equivalent when the actual charge is annual.
**Confidence:** Medium

---

### Pattern: Objection: "Too expensive" usually means "I don't see the value yet"
**Evidence:**
**Application:** Don't discount immediately. Ask: "What were you expecting?" then reframe value before adjusting price.
**Confidence:** Medium

## Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern: Hiding the price
**Evidence:** Requiring a call to get pricing increases friction, reduces qualified leads in many contexts.
**Exception:** Enterprise deals where price is genuinely custom.

### Anti-Pattern: Too many tiers
**Evidence:** More than 4 tiers causes analysis paralysis.
